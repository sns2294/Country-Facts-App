Political map of Central America and the Caribbean

21 countries, each colored differently from its neighbors. Dots mark capital cities. Territories are in their parent country's color, with the parent in parentheses; an inset enlarges the Lesser Antilles.

Tap the map to open it full size, then pinch to zoom.Click the map to open it full screen, then scroll to zoom and drag to move.

Borders: Natural Earth (public domain). Capital cities: Natural Earth populated places. Drawn in the Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection. Belize's border with Guatemala is the internationally recognized line; Guatemala claims part of Belize, and the dispute is pending before the International Court of Justice. Areas claimed by more than one country are given to none and marked, not to scale: Navassa Island (administered by the United States, claimed by Haiti), Isla Aves (administered by Venezuela, claimed by others) and the Serranilla and Bajo Nuevo banks (administered by Colombia, claimed by several countries). Guantánamo Bay, which the United States leases from Cuba, is shown as part of Cuba. The Dutch territories are not in the Netherlands' Europe-map color, because Sint Maarten shares an island with French Saint Martin.

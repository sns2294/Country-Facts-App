Political map of Oceania

14 countries, each colored differently from its neighbors. Dots mark capital cities. Territories are in their parent country's color, with the parent in parentheses; the small island states and territories are labeled with a pointer line.

Borders: Natural Earth (public domain). Capital cities: Natural Earth populated places. Drawn in the Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection, centered on the Pacific so that nothing is split at the 180° line. Hawaii, a U.S. state, is shown because it falls in the frame. The U.S. Minor Outlying Islands are a scattering of small, mostly uninhabited islands and reefs (Wake Island, Johnston Atoll, Palmyra Atoll, Kingman Reef, Jarvis, Baker and Howland); they are in the United States' color but too small to see, and one label stands for them all, at Palmyra. Tokelau is in New Zealand's color: the map data draws two of its three atolls (Fakaofo and Atafu) as part of New Zealand's shape, and the dot marks the third, Nukunonu, which it lacks. Matthew and Hunter Islands are administered by France and claimed by Vanuatu. The Minerva Reefs are claimed by both Tonga and Fiji. The map assigns none of them to a country: Matthew and Hunter are shown as one hatched mark, not to scale, with Matthew Island cut out of New Caledonia's shape, and the Minerva Reefs, which are not in the map data, are not drawn. The Coral Sea Islands and the Ashmore and Cartier Islands are in Australia's color but too small to see. New Zealand's subantarctic islands and Australia's Macquarie Island lie beyond the bottom edge, and Christmas Island and the Cocos (Keeling) Islands beyond the left edge.
